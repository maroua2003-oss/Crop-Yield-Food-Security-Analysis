# Executive Summary

## 1. Project Overview
This project analyzes agricultural production trends in Sub-Saharan Africa using FAOSTAT data covering 2015–2024.

## 2. Objectives
- Analyze agricultural production and yield trends.
- Identify countries and crops experiencing declining yields.
- Rank countries according to food security risk indicators.
- Identify priority countries for targeted agricultural interventions.

## 3. Methodology
The analysis uses agricultural yield trends and average production volume (2022–2024) to assess potential food security risks.

## 4. Key Findings
- Risk is highly concentrated by country: Uganda (167,614 t) and Côte d'Ivoire (124,494 t) account for ~61% of the 478,271 tonnes at risk per year, and the top 10 countries represent ~93% of total risk.
- Roots, tubers and pulses dominate crop losses: Yams (149,229 t), Beans, dry (91,132 t), Cassava (77,701 t) and Sweet potatoes (66,798 t) together make up ~79% of total tonnes at risk.
- There is a steep drop-off after the top 10 countries: risk falls from 9,268 t (Kenya, rank 10) to 6,221 t (Somalia, rank 11), then below 2,700 t from rank 12 onward.
- Many crops show minimal exposure: Rice (0.0 t), Fonio (5.0 t), Lentils (53.5 t), Wheat (203.8 t) and Broad beans (254.8 t), so interventions should focus on a small group of high-risk crops.
  
## 5. Priority Countries

The three highest-ranked countries are Uganda, Côte d'Ivoire and Zambia.

- Uganda (rank 1, 167,614 t at risk per year): it has the highest exposure by a wide margin, representing roughly 35% of the total 478,271 tonnes at risk.
- Côte d'Ivoire (rank 2, 124,494 t at risk per year): it is the second most exposed country and, together with Uganda, accounts for ~61% of total risk.
- Zambia (rank 3, 30,912 t at risk per year): its exposure is much lower than the top two, but it leads the remaining group of countries (Mozambique, Sudan, Cameroon, etc.), which each have between 9,000 and 30,000 t at risk.

Ranking is based on the sum of tonnes at risk per year, so these countries are the ones where interventions would protect the largest volume of production.

## 6. Recommendations

- Focus mitigation efforts first on Uganda and Côte d'Ivoire, since they concentrate about 61% of total tonnes at risk.
- Extend the programme to the rest of the top 10 countries (Zambia, Mozambique, Sudan, Cameroon, Guinea, Chad, Rwanda, Kenya), which together cover ~93% of the risk.
- Prioritise the highest-risk crops: yams, dry beans, cassava and sweet potatoes (~79% of total tonnes at risk), followed by soya beans, potatoes, groundnuts and sorghum.
- Deprioritise crops with minimal exposure (rice, fonio, lentils, wheat, broad beans) to avoid spreading resources too thinly.
- Re-run the analysis regularly and validate the results against the full dataset, as the figures here come from the visible rows of the pivot tables.
